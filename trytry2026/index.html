Why map small & micro enterprises?

Without a clear picture of the local business landscape, support programs risk being misdirected, underutilized, or inequitable.

Targeted support

Identify which sectors and areas need the most assistance — from sari-sari stores to home-based food producers.

  • Locate unregistered businesses
  • Spot high-potential clusters

Evidence-based policy

Turn raw data into insights: employment size, sector, capital, and growth barriers.

  • Prioritize funding & training
  • Monitor program impact

Inclusive growth

Reach the underserved — women-led, youth, and rural micro-enterprises often invisible in formal statistics.

  • Bridge the formalization gap
  • Promote equitable recovery

What an MSME map includes

A robust mapping framework goes beyond pins on a map — it captures the socio-economic identity of each enterprise.

Enterprise profile

Name, owner, sector, years in operation, registration status (formal/informal).

Employment & size

Number of workers, full-time/part-time, family vs. hired labor.

Financials & needs

Estimated monthly revenue, access to credit, training needs, and growth barriers.

Geospatial data

Barangay, street, GPS coordinates, proximity to markets or transport.

Compliance & permits

Mayor’s permit, DTI/SEC registration, BIR, and other LGU-specific requirements.

Dynamic updates

Regular re-surveys to capture new businesses, closures, and changing needs.

From mapping to LGU program

A step-by-step approach used by local government units to turn data into action.

1. Planning & profiling

Define scope, form enumerator teams, and design the survey tool.

2. Field enumeration

House-to-house visits, GPS tagging, and digital data capture.

3. Data consolidation

Clean, verify, and store in a centralized MSME database.

4. Analysis & mapping

Generate heat maps, sector reports, and priority areas.

5. Program design

Co-create support: grants, training, market linkage, and policy reforms.
